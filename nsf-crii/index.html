NSF-CRII

NSF CRII project on spectrum- and energy-efficient large-scale IoT communications.

CRII: CNS: Towards Spectrum and Energy Efficient Large-scale IoT Communications: A Cross-layer Optimization Approach

Personnel

Principal Investigator: Haijian Sun, Assistant Professor

Students: Hanwen Zhang, Paul Kudyba, Xiaona Gao, John Song, and Sterling Strohauer.

Project Description

Recent years have seen rapid growth in Internet of Things (IoT) devices for healthcare, entertainment, monitoring, smart homes, and smart infrastructure. Enabling ubiquitous connectivity, massive traffic, and sustainable deployment requires higher spectrum efficiency and energy efficiency than conventional active communication or passive ambient backscatter communication can provide alone.

This project improves both spectrum and energy efficiency while providing quality-of-service guarantees for large-scale IoT networks. The approach uses a Wireless Powered Hybrid Communication (WPHC) structure that integrates passive and active communication, harvests ambient energy, and supports efficient spectrum utilization through user cooperation. SunLab studies cross-layer physical-layer and MAC-layer designs, plus software and hardware testbeds for evaluation.

REU and Broader Impacts

John Song joined SunLab as an REU student in Spring 2025 and is working on wireless channel simulation. Paul Kudyba led SunLab’s efforts in NSF AERPAW’s Find a Rover Challenge, where the team ranked second and third in the final competition. Undergraduate student Sterling Strohauer designed an FM backscatter communication system and presented it at UGA ECE’s student EXPO.
